# Project governance

RefKit currently uses maintainer-led governance. The goal is clear ownership and a small decision surface while the project is early.

## Roles

- Users run RefKit or its packages and provide feedback.
- Contributors submit issues, documentation, code, tests, or reviews under the applicable license and DCO.
- Maintainers review contributions, set product direction, manage releases, moderate community spaces, and protect security and legal boundaries.
- Repository administrators manage protected settings, credentials, publishing identities, and maintainer access.

Maintainer status is granted by existing maintainers based on sustained, trustworthy contributions and the needs of the project. It can be removed for inactivity, security reasons, policy violations, or loss of trust.

## Decisions

Maintainers seek practical consensus for ordinary changes. Repository administrators have final responsibility when consensus is not reached, when a decision affects product direction, or when security, legal, privacy, or release risk requires a prompt decision.

Material technical and product decisions should be recorded in an issue, pull request, or living project document. Security-sensitive, personal, contractual, and credential-related details may remain private.

The maintainers may change roadmap, release cadence, support scope, or governance as the project grows. Governance changes should be proposed publicly before adoption unless an urgent safety or legal concern prevents advance discussion.

## Contributions and releases

All external contributions require DCO sign-off. A maintainer approves and merges public changes. A repository administrator authorizes releases and applies the publication checklist. No single approval bypasses required security, legal, or artifact gates.

The license permits forks subject to its terms. Forks do not become official RefKit projects and must follow [TRADEMARKS.md](./TRADEMARKS.md).
